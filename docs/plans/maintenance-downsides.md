## The problem

The cell can turn four kinds of maintenance down as species fall (the regulation cogs; upstream switches
`reg_on_<species><suffix>`, all off by default; `FermenterCell.MaintenanceRegulation`): protein synthesis, RNA and
lipid upkeep, repair, and the maintenance ATPase. Regulation only acts below rest (f = 1 at and above it). Two have a
modelled cost: cutting protein synthesis lets proteins run down, cutting repair lets damage (four types since
2026-10-15) build up and kill. **Two are free**: cutting RNA and lipid upkeep loses nothing (the mRNA, ribosome and
lipid counts are fixed parameters), and cutting the maintenance ATPase (everything not itemised: `cytosol__v_hyd`, the
largest item) saves ATP for nothing. The level-1 "mediocre" reference player wins mostly through that free cut.

Starving bacteria do cut maintenance severalfold (the stringent response), so cutting must stay the right move in a
famine; it must not be free.

## The three changes (all upstream, in the cell's CellML)

1. **RNA upkeep -> ribosomes are lost and must be rebuilt.** Make the ribosome count a state of biosynthesis,
   `n_ribo` (now a parameter, 7,000/um3 x volume). Unpaid rRNA upkeep degrades ribosomes:
   `dn_ribo/dt = k_ribo * n_ribo * (R_reg_upkeep * f_ATP_bio - 1)` below rest (net loss at the share not paid), plus
   rebuilding towards the resting count when upkeep is back to full, paid at de novo cost (`c_nt_denovo` ~50 ~P per
   nt, x `L_rRNA` 4,566 nt, plus the ribosomal proteins at `c_syn + c_poly` per aa). Protein-building capacity follows
   the ribosomes: `build_capacity` in protein_expression becomes `build_capacity_rest * n_ribo / n_ribo_rest` (a new
   port from bio to protein carrying `n_ribo`, or a ratio `x_ribo`). Do the same for mRNA if it is cheap to do
   (`n_mRNA`, half-life 5 min, so unpaid mRNA upkeep empties the pool in minutes and protein synthesis with it); if
   that makes regulation useless, keep mRNA fixed and say why in the module notes. The gameplay this gives: cut deep
   in a famine and survive, then recover slowly (a lag) when food returns.
2. **Lipid upkeep -> envelope damage.** The unpaid share of lipid turnover becomes physical (envelope) damage:
   `R_dmg_phys += k_lipid_dmg * (1 - R_reg_upkeep * f_ATP_bio)` (only the lipid part of the upkeep; split the upkeep
   switch per item only if needed). Optionally raise the passive leaks with `D_phys` (a leakier membrane); decide
   with the designer before adding that, since it touches every leak module.
3. **Maintenance ATPase -> lapsed quality control.** The unpaid share of the maintenance ATPase (cytosol's `R_reg` on
   `v_hyd`) becomes ribosome-and-protein damage: `R_dmg_ribo += k_qc_dmg * (1 - R_reg_maint)`. This needs the
   maintenance's regulation factor in biosynthesis (a port from cytosol, or compute the deficit where both are seen:
   the fermenter supermodule links `cytosol` and `bio`). Choose `k_qc_dmg` so a full cut through a long famine (hours)
   costs a noticeable but repairable share of a lethal load, e.g. 20-30% over 6 h; it is a placeholder either way.

Every new value is a placeholder: log it in `docs/review/unreferenced-values.md` (rule in memory/CLAUDE.md). Write
module notes in the present tense (no history).
